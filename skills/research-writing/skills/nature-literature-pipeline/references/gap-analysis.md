# Checking for prior work

Use this procedure when a reader asks whether a specific research question, system or combination has been studied.

1. **Define the question and search scope.** Identify the subject, related names and spellings, comparator or component terms where relevant, date boundary and sources available. If the reader limits the task to a collection, search only that collection.
2. **Search and record coverage.** Use exact and broader queries, synonyms, and adjacent work to find alternate terminology or prior versions. Log the database or supplied collection, date, exact query, filters, hits inspected and any access limits. Identify separately what could be checked in metadata, abstracts and full text.
3. **Classify by relevance.** For each direct or adjacent result, record the citation key or DOI, actual subject, method, finding and source location. For a multi-component material, for example, a paper on a subset of components provides adjacent evidence; check alternate names for the full combination before drawing a conclusion.
4. **Write the finding at the supported scope.** A direct match identifies prior work. With zero direct matches, report “We found no directly matching study in [sources] using [queries] through [date].” Give the closest studies, unresolved candidates and coverage limitations. A claim about why a topic is understudied needs its own evidence.

For a manuscript, hand the search log, candidate sources and proposed wording to `$research-writing literature verify` when available. Otherwise provide them to the author for verification before use in the introduction. Record inaccessible sources explicitly and keep the conclusion bounded by the material actually examined.
